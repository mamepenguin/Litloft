## Summary

The intelligence addon picks "representative scenes" of a video in one shared function,
`select_candidates`, used by the Visual Index, by a file question about a video with no
text (up to 8 frames), and by `GET /files/{id}/scenes` behind the MCP tool
`list_video_scenes`. Today it takes `clamp(6, ceil(duration / 300), 24)` scene-CLIP
candidates nearest the centres of equal time buckets, so a video up to 30 minutes always
gets 6 nearly evenly spaced frames, and a talk or screen recording, whose scene filter
barely fires, only has its 30-second interval frames to choose from.

This change replaces the selection with one that follows the video's changes, measured on
14 varied videos (talks with slides, screen recordings, anime, a film, a music video,
timelapses, home videos, a dash cam, an English video, a 1-minute short):

- **SPEC-ADDON-111, frame scan at index time.** Every native video is decoded once at one
  frame per second, small and grey. Per second the scan keeps a 256-bit difference hash
  and the picture's JPEG size; the seconds where the picture changed (the frame-OCR rule)
  become extra candidates, embedded with the CLIP model and stored in their own vector
  table, `vec_clip_change`, which nothing but the selection reads. Already indexed videos
  are scanned in the background.
- **SPEC-ADDON-112, the selection.** The video is cut into sections at the strongest
  visual changes and transcript topic changes, with a coverage guarantee; one frame per
  section is chosen to be typical of its section, unlike frames already chosen, and not a
  momentary or near-empty picture; adjacent sections showing the same thing are merged.
  Whether two frames show "the same thing" is decided by their on-screen text when both
  carry a lot of it, otherwise by CLIP.
- **SPEC-ADDON-113, the addon's consumers.** A scene keeps its frame time in `start_time`
  and gains the section it represents (`section_start`, `section_end`). The Visual Index
  waits for the frame scan, keeps old runs until the user regenerates them, and plays a
  scene from its section start; the scenes route returns sections with their frame times; a file question about a
  text-less video takes its frames from the deduplicated list; the indexing dashboard shows
  the scan.
- **SPEC-CORE-110, the MCP tool and docs.** `list_video_scenes` shows up to 40 sections,
  each with its section and frame time, and fetches each frame at its frame time.

Decided by the human (2026-11-15): change frames are kept apart from scene search; the
navigation density is one section per 75 seconds, aimed at 8 to 40 sections before merges and never more than 40; a scene plays from its section
start, and the scene card shows that time; already indexed videos are scanned
automatically; old Visual Index runs are kept until regenerated by hand; the Visual Index
waits for CLIP and the scan only; `list_video_scenes` keeps its 45 s frame budget; scans of
new videos and of regenerates go ahead of the backfill; a scan whose frames cannot be taken
is closed after one attempt; automatic runs after a scan start only for videos with no run;
the indexing widget gains a progress bar for the scan.

## Terms

- **Native video**: an `indexed_files` row whose `mime_type` is in `VIDEO_TYPES`. A `.loft`
  is not one.
- **Scene candidate**: an `embeddings` row of the file with `embedding_type='clip'`, vector
  in `vec_clip`.
- **Change frame**: an `embeddings` row of the file with `embedding_type='clip_change'`,
  vector in `vec_clip_change`.
- **Pool**: the frames the selection chooses from (112 step 1), ordered by time, then type
  (`clip` before `clip_change`), then embedding id.
- **Span** `[lo, hi)`: with a window, its start (0 when absent) and its end; a window
  without an end uses the duration; with no window, `[0, duration)`. When `hi` is still
  unknown it is the last scene-candidate or change-frame time at or after `lo`, plus 1 s,
  taken before the pool is filtered to the span. A window starting at or after
  a known duration has an empty result.
- **Rank** of a value among `n` values: sort ascending, ties by earlier time; the value at
  0-based position `p` has rank `(p + 1) / n`.
- **Segment text** at time `t`: the frame-OCR segment of `video_frame_texts` with
  `start_time ≤ t < end_time`; its chunks are the rows sharing that `start_time` and
  `end_time`. Its **length** is the length of its longest chunk; its **bigrams** are the
  union of the character bigrams of its chunks with whitespace removed.
- **Median** of the scanned sizes: the middle value of the sorted sizes, or the mean of the
  two middle values for an even count.

## Required items

### 1. Normal flow

**SPEC-ADDON-111, frame scan.**

1. The migration adds `indexed_files.frame_scan_indexed` (`BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`) and,
   in the same step, sets it to 1 for every row that is not a native video. Later rows that
   are not native videos are marked done where the indexer marks other inapplicable flags.
2. A native video with the flag false is queued as a `frame_scan` task on its own queue,
   served by one worker (one scan at a time). A scan queued for a newly added video runs at
   priority 0, one queued by a CLIP regenerate at 100, and the backfill of already indexed
   videos (startup resume and sweeps) at −10, frame OCR's resumed priority, so new videos and
   regenerates go ahead of the backfill. When a task starts and the flag is already true, the
   row is gone or inactive, or the file is no longer a native video, it does nothing. It
   honours the indexer's pause as frame OCR does. When the task starts, the file's
   drive must have the `index` feature on; when it is off, nothing is written and the flag
   stays false. A failed policy lookup counts as on, as the indexer's other policy checks do.
3. The worker decodes the first video stream with ffmpeg at `fps=1`, scaled to 160 px wide
   keeping the displayed aspect ratio (ffmpeg's rotation applied, even height), grey,
   holding no database session. 120 s without decoded bytes is a stall: ffmpeg is killed.
4. For each decoded second `s` (0, 1, 2, ...) it computes the 256-bit difference hash (the
   picture reduced to 17x16 grey, each pixel compared with its right neighbour, as
   `frame_ocr._dhash` does) and the byte size of that 160-wide grey picture encoded as JPEG
   quality 75.
5. Change seconds are second 0 and then every second whose hash differs from the last
   change second's hash by at least 16 bits and is at least 10 s after it.
6. When there are more change seconds than `indexing.frame_extraction.max_frames`, that many
   are kept, spread over the whole list: the ones at 0-based positions
   `floor(i · n / max_frames)` for `i = 0 .. max_frames − 1`, where `n` is the count.
7. For each kept change second the worker takes the frame at that second the way scene-CLIP
   interval frames are taken and embeds it with `clip.embed_image` (the CLIP worker's loaded
   model). This happens before any write. Extraction and embedding together stop after
   600 s; the change frames embedded by then are the ones written.
8. It checks that the file is still reachable; if not, nothing is written. Then, in one
   transaction that first re-reads the `indexed_files` row with `active` true (and writes
   nothing when it is gone or inactive), it deletes the file's previous change frames, their
   vectors and its previous scan, writes one `embeddings` row per embedded change frame
   (`embedding_type='clip_change'`, `vector_table='vec_clip_change'`,
   `id = "chg_" + file_id + "_" + <second>`, `timestamp_start` = the second) with its vector,
   writes the `video_frame_scans` row, and sets the flag true.
9. Regenerating **Image analysis (CLIP)** of a native video (`reindex` with task `clip`)
   also queues the scan, claiming `(file_id, "frame_scan")` in the in-flight set, also when
   the CLIP task itself answers `already_queued`. When no scan of the file is running (in the
   scan worker's processing list or claimed), the flag is set false; when one is queued but
   not running, nothing more is queued; when one is running, nothing changes and its result
   stands. `is_queued` and the reindex task map gain `frame_scan` for this. The response body is
   unchanged and reports the CLIP task only. The old change frames and scan stay in use until
   the new scan replaces them.
10. `prioritize` on a file, which a manual Visual Index request uses, also bumps its pending
    scan.

`video_frame_scans` (format version 1): `file_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY`, `version INTEGER`,
`seconds INTEGER`, `hashes BLOB` (`seconds × 32` bytes: for each second the 256 comparison
bits in row-major order, packed most significant bit first, as `numpy.packbits`), `sizes
BLOB` (`seconds × 2` bytes: unsigned 16-bit little-endian, values above 65535 stored as
65535), `created_at`. A row of another version, or whose blobs do not have the lengths its
`seconds` gives, is read as no scan.

**SPEC-ADDON-112, the selection.** A pure function over stored rows; it reads no video.

Inputs: the file's scene candidates, change frames, scan (optional), its transcript
embeddings (`embedding_type='whisper'`, each at its `timestamp_start`, ordered by that time
and then embedding id, optional), its
frame-OCR rows (optional), and the span.

1. **Pool.** The scene candidates, plus every change frame more than 3 s from every scene
   candidate, keeping those with a vector and a time in the span. An empty pool gives an
   empty result.
2. **Section count.** `K = min(clamp(8, ceil((hi − lo) / 75), 40), pool size)`;
   `m = (hi − lo) / (3K)`.
3. **Boundary candidates.**
   - Visual: for each pair of consecutive pool frames, the distance `1 − cos(a, b)`, where
     `a` is the mean vector of up to two frames ending with the earlier one and `b` of up to
     two frames starting with the later one. The boundary is at the later frame's time and
     its visual rank is the rank of its distance among all pairs.
   - Topic, when more than 6 transcript embeddings fall in the span: the same over
     consecutive transcript embeddings with windows of up to three, at the later
     embedding's time, with its topic rank.
   - Joining: each topic boundary of topic rank at least 0.7 that has a visual boundary of
     visual rank at least 0.8 within 5 s joins the highest-ranked such visual boundary (the
     earlier on a tie) and stops being a boundary of its own. A visual boundary with joiners
     takes the highest joiner topic rank (the earlier on a tie) as its topic rank, scores
     `max(visual rank, topic rank) + 0.25`, and has kind `visual_topic`.
   - Every other boundary scores its own rank and has kind `visual` or `topic`.
   - A boundary in the first `max(20 s, 3 %)` or the last `max(30 s, 6 %)` of the span has
     its score multiplied by 0.3.
   - A boundary's **topic strength** is its topic rank; 0 for kinds `visual`, `coverage` and
     for the span start (kind `start`).
4. **Cutting.** Every boundary below must leave at least one pool frame on each side within
   its section.
   - Boundaries are taken by score, highest first, the earlier on a tie, up to `K − 1`,
     skipping one closer than `m` to `lo`, to `hi` or to a boundary already taken.
   - Then, while there are fewer than `min(ceil(1.5 · K), 40)` sections and some section is
     longer than `2.5 · (hi − lo) / K`, the longest such section (the earlier on a tie) is
     split at its highest-scoring boundary at least `m` from both of its ends (keeping that
     boundary's kind); or, with none, at the pool frame nearest its midpoint (the earlier on a
     tie) at least `m` from both ends, with kind `coverage`. A section that has neither is
     left whole and the next longest is tried.
5. **Representative.** Sections are visited longest first, the earlier on a tie. A section's
   eligible frames are its pool frames in `[start + 2 s, end)`, or all its pool frames when
   there are none. Each is scored by its cosine to the mean vector of the eligible frames
   minus 0.7 × its highest cosine to a representative already chosen; ties go to the earlier
   frame in pool order. Of the four best, the first that has a steady second is taken at
   that second; with no scan, or when none of the four has one, the best is taken at its own
   time.
6. **Steady second.** For a frame at time `t`: the first of `ceil(t)`, `ceil(t) + 1`,
   `ceil(t) + 2` that is before the section's end, is in the scan, has its next second in the
   scan, has a hash differing from the next second's by fewer than 24 bits, and has a JPEG
   size at least 0.45 × the median of all the file's scanned sizes. The representative's
   **frame time** is that second; its vector and embedding id stay those of the pool frame.
7. **Textual and same.** A representative is textual when the segment text at its frame time
   has a length of at least 200. Two representatives show the same thing at cosine `c` when
   both are textual and the Jaccard similarity of their bigrams is at least 0.85, or neither
   is textual and the cosine of their vectors is at least `c`; never when exactly one is
   textual.
8. **Navigation list.** Walking the sections in time order, a section is merged into the one
   before it when its own start boundary's topic strength is below 0.7 and the two
   representatives show the same thing at 0.93. The merged section spans both and keeps the
   earlier section's kind. Its representative is whichever of the two representatives came
   from the longer section as cut in step 4 (the earlier on a tie); a chain of merges keeps
   comparing the representative held so far, by the length of the section it was chosen in.
9. **Ask list.** From the navigation list, longest section first (the earlier on a tie), a
   section is dropped when its representative shows the same thing at 0.95 as one already
   kept; the rest are returned in time order.

Each list item carries the section start, section end, frame time, the pool frame's
embedding id and type, the section's kind and whether it is textual.

**SPEC-ADDON-113, the addon's consumers.**

1. **Visual Index readiness.** A native video is accepted for a run, manual or automatic,
   only when it has scene candidates and `frame_scan_indexed` is true. Until then a request
   answers the existing `waiting_clip` reason, whose text ("waiting on scene indexing")
   stays. The existing hook that runs after a CLIP pass is also run after a frame scan, so
   with `video_visual_index` on `on_index` a new video's run is enqueued when the later of
   the two finishes. The hook runs after every scan that sets the flag true. Whisper and
   frame OCR are not waited for. An automatic request made by the hook after a scan creates a
   run only for a video that has no run at all; it never replaces an active run of an older
   pipeline version, and never retries a failed run (those stay for the user's Regenerate or
   Retry). The automatic request after a CLIP pass keeps today's rule for runs of version 3.
   `_build_scenes`, which uses the new selection, stamps the run with version 3 when it builds
   it, also for a run queued before the upgrade.
2. **Visual Index scenes.** A run's scenes are the navigation list of the whole file, in time
   order, with `start_time` = frame time, `end_time` null as today, new nullable columns
   `section_start` and `section_end` = the section, and `clip_embedding_id` = the
   representative's embedding id. Everything that reads `start_time` today (the frame sent to
   the vision model, the prompt's time, the ±7.5 s transcript excerpt, the scene text
   embedding, exact search, Find in file, `/search/scenes`) keeps reading it unchanged.
   `GET /visual-index` scene items carry `section_start` and `section_end`.
   `PIPELINE_VERSION` becomes 3, and the candidate fingerprint covers the pool's scene
   candidates and change frames in pool order.
3. **Scenes panel.** A scene's **seek time** is its `section_start`, or its `start_time` when
   that is null. The ruler marks, the choice of the scene shown on the card (the last one at
   or before the point being looked at), the card's time label and the seek on click all use
   the seek time. A scene with `section_start` marks the grid tile whose range contains its
   seek time (from the tile's start up to the next tile's start; the first tile also takes a
   seek time before it); a scene without it keeps today's rule (the tile whose start is
   within 0.05 s of its `start_time`).
4. **`GET /files/{id}/scenes`.** Returns the navigation list over the span: each item's
   `start` and `end` are the section bounds, a new field `frame_time` is the frame time, and
   `transcript_excerpt` is the existing ±7.5 s window around `frame_time`. `candidate_count`
   is the pool size. At most 40 items.
5. **File question about a video with no text.** Terms step 1 of SPEC-ADDON-108 takes the Ask
   list's frame times instead of `select_candidates`; removal within 1 s of the position,
   index thinning and evenly spaced filling are unchanged.
6. **Indexing dashboard.** `/status` gains `indexed.frame_scan` (active native videos with
   the flag true) and `pending.frame_scan` (active native videos with the flag false), the
   latter counted in `pending.total`. The indexing status widget draws a progress bar for the
   pair, in the same component and order as the other tasks, and the `frame_scan` task is a
   row of the queue breakdown; both are labelled "Scene change scan" / 「場面の切り替わり」 with
   the lucide icon `ScanLine`. No design artifact is made: the bar and row reuse the widget's
   existing parts, and the card's time label changes value, not appearance.

**SPEC-CORE-110, MCP and docs.**

1. `list_video_scenes` describes "up to 40 representative scenes", requests each scene's
   small frame at `frame_time`, and labels each scene `Scene <index> at <start>s (frame at
   <frame_time>s)`, its image right after the label. Each item of its summary JSON gains
   `frame_time`; `end` is always a number. The 4-in-flight, 45 s frame budget is unchanged;
   frames it cannot fetch in time are reported as today.
2. `mcp-server/README.md` and `docs/addons/intelligence.md` state the new counts, the frame
   time, the frame scan, and that the Visual Index waits for it; the Visual Index passage
   says scenes are marked and played at their section start; the `frame_extraction` config
   passage says `max_frames` also caps change frames per video.

### 2. Failure cases

| Where | What | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Scan start | the drive's `index` feature is off | nothing is written; the flag stays false |
| Scan | the file is unreachable at the start or at the check before the write (step 8), including after some change frames were extracted | nothing is written; the flag stays false; a later sweep retries |
| Scan | the file was trashed, went missing or was purged during the scan | nothing is written |
| Scan | the CLIP model cannot be loaded | nothing is written; the flag stays false |
| Scan | the model is loaded but every change frame fails to extract or embed | the scan row is written with no change frames; the flag is set; only a CLIP regenerate tries again |
| Scan | the 600 s extraction limit is reached | the change frames embedded so far are written with the scan row; the flag is set |
| Scan | the step 8 transaction fails | nothing is written; the flag stays false; a later sweep retries |
| Scan | a stall, or ffmpeg exits early or non-zero, after at least one second was read, the file still reachable | the seconds read are stored as the scan, with their change frames; the flag is set |
| Scan | no second read at all, the file reachable (no video stream, undecodable) | the file's previous scan row and change frames, if any, are deleted; the flag is set |
| Scan | one change frame cannot be extracted or embedded | it is skipped; the rest are written |
| Selection | no scan, no transcript, no frame OCR, no duration, no change frames | each is optional; the selection runs as stated |
| File question | reading the selection's inputs raises | unchanged: `scene_candidates_failed` is logged and the even fill runs |

### 3. States

- `indexed_files.frame_scan_indexed`: false → true when scanned, not a native video, or
  unreadable. Back to false only on a CLIP regenerate of the file (111.9) and, for every
  native video, when `vec_clip_change` is rebuilt. Other reindex tasks, a move, a recovery
  from missing and a rename leave it as it is.
- The scan row and the change frames: absent → present; replaced together by a rescan;
  deleted with the file. At startup `vec_clip_change`'s own vector dimension is compared with
  the configured CLIP dimension, independently of `vec_clip`; on a mismatch every change
  frame is deleted, `vec_clip_change` is recreated and every native video's flag is set
  false. The scan rows stay and are replaced by the rescans.
- Visual Index: a run made before this change is stale (pipeline version), shows the
  existing stale state and stays until the user regenerates it. A video waiting for its CLIP
  pass or scan shows the existing waiting state.

### 4. Data read and written

- Written: `indexed_files.frame_scan_indexed` (new column), `video_frame_scans` (new table),
  `embeddings` rows of type `clip_change`, `vec_clip_change` (new vec0 table with
  `vec_clip`'s dimension and the same partition-key DDL as `vec_clip`),
  `video_visual_scenes.section_start` and `section_end` (new nullable columns),
  `video_visual_runs` as today, frame cache entries at frame times. The ORM declaration of
  `embeddings.vector_table` widens from `String(10)` to `String(20)` to hold
  `vec_clip_change`; SQLite does not enforce the length, so no migration is needed.
- Read: `embeddings`, `vec_clip` (`clip`), `vec_clip_change`, `vec_text` (`whisper`),
  `video_frame_texts`, `video_frame_scans`, `indexed_files`.
- Only additions: no table or column is dropped, renamed or given a new meaning. A build from
  before this change reads none of the new tables or columns, and its queries filtered by
  `embedding_type` never ask for `clip_change`.
- Nothing in the core database changes.

### 5. External services

None in the scan or the selection; both run in the container. The Visual Index's vision
calls are unchanged in kind; a run can now make up to 40 (it was 24).

### 6. Authorization

Unchanged. The scenes route keeps `require_drive` and its file-access check; the Visual Index
routes and the file question keep theirs. The scan has no route and runs only for a drive
whose `index` feature is on.

### 7. Effect on existing features

- Scene search, tag kNN, the Scenes grid tiles (`clip-timestamps`), scene segments and their
  drive threshold, similar files and concept tagging read `vec_clip` and the `clip` /
  `clip_thumbnail` rows only, and are unchanged.
- Visual Index: up to 40 scenes (aimed at 8 to 40 before merges) chosen by the new
  selection, each with its section; a run
  starts only after CLIP and the scan are done; every existing run shows as stale and stays
  until regenerated by hand.
- Exact search, Find in file, scene text embeddings and `/search/scenes` keep reporting a
  scene at its frame time (`start_time`), as today.
- File question frames for a text-less video follow the new selection.
- `list_video_scenes` and the scenes route return up to 40 sections with a frame time.
- `.loft` (YouTube) references are not scanned; their selection uses storyboard candidates
  and the transcript. Their `indexed_files.duration` is null, so the span falls back as
  defined. Scanning YouTube is a later change.
- Purge, drive purge, orphan cleanup, the vector-table allow-list and the partition repair
  cover `vec_clip_change` and the scan rows.

### 8. Error behavior

- A failed, stalled, skipped or policy-blocked scan is logged at warning with its failure
  class and file id; a skipped change frame with its second. Nothing is shown to the user.
- Users see no new error text. A video waiting for its inputs shows the Visual Index's
  existing waiting text; a stale run the existing stale state.

### 9. User-visible behavior

- Scenes panel: more described scenes, spread by the video's changes; marks on the tile
  containing each scene's section start; the card shows and plays from the section start.
  Runs made before this change are drawn exactly as before (stale) until regenerated by hand.
- A newly indexed video's Visual Index (on `on_index`) starts after its CLIP pass and its
  scan, with no restart. A transcript or frame OCR that arrives later is used by a run only
  after it is regenerated.
- Asking about a video with no text sends frames from the new selection.
- MCP `list_video_scenes`: up to 40 scenes, each with its section and frame time, the image
  being the frame at the frame time; with 40 scenes some frames may come back as
  `time budget exceeded` within the unchanged 45 s budget.
- The indexing dashboard shows a "Scene change scan" queue row while scans are pending.
- **Index details** has no row for the scan; **Regenerate** of **Image analysis (CLIP)** also
  rescans.

### 10. Non-functional

- The scan decodes each native video once more (seconds to a few minutes of CPU in the
  samples) and embeds at most `max_frames` change frames (500 by default; 7 to 563 change
  seconds in the samples, 4 to 484 left after dropping those within 3 s of a scene candidate).
- `video_frame_scans` stores 34 bytes per second (about 122 KB per hour of video).
- The selection runs at request time in the scenes route and the file question, reading
  stored rows and doing numpy work over at most a few thousand vectors; it decodes no video.
- A Visual Index run makes up to 40 vision calls (local or off-host per the drive's
  `llm_cloud` policy, unchanged). The scan calls the CLIP worker's loaded model through
  `clip.embed_image`; no new lock is added, as the CLIP worker already calls it from its own
  thread.

## Invariants

I1. Scene search, tag kNN, scene segments, similar files, concept tagging and the Scenes grid tiles return the same results for a file before and after its frame scan; no change-frame vector is ever in `vec_clip`.
I2. For the same stored inputs, given in any row order, the selection returns the same navigation and Ask lists, item for item.
I3. Navigation sections cover the span without gaps or overlaps, in time order; each holds at least one pool frame; every frame time lies inside its own section (`start ≤ frame_time < end`); no two sections share a pool frame.
I4. With a non-empty pool the navigation list has at least one and at most 40 items; the sections as cut number at most `min(ceil(1.5 · K), 40)`; a section as cut is longer than `2.5 · span / K` only when that limit is reached or it has no boundary and no pool frame at least `m` from both ends that leaves a pool frame on each side.
I5. No two items of the Ask list show the same thing at 0.95; two textual representatives whose bigram Jaccard is below 0.85 are never merged or dropped for each other, and a textual and a non-textual one never are, whatever their cosine.
I6. A representative never sits on a second whose hash differs from the next second's by 24 bits or more, or whose size is below 0.45 × the median, while one of its section's four best frames has a steady second inside the section; with no scan it sits at its pool frame's own time.
I7. A frame scan writes its scan row, its change frames with their vectors and its flag in one transaction or writes nothing; it writes nothing and leaves the flag false when the file is unreachable at the start or before the write, the drive's `index` feature is off, the CLIP model cannot be loaded or the write fails, and writes nothing when the file is no longer active; it keeps what it read after a stall, an early exit or the 600 s extraction limit of a reachable file, and sets the flag even when no change frame could be taken; its change seconds are second 0 and each second at least 16 bits and 10 s from the previous change second, at most `max_frames` of them spread as step 6 states.
I8. Only native videos are ever scanned, and every active native video with the flag false is queued, new videos and regenerates ahead of the backfill; purging a file and the orphan cleanup remove its scan row, its change frames and their vectors; a `vec_clip_change` dimension mismatch removes every change frame and sets every native video's flag false; a CLIP regenerate of a native video queues one scan and sets its flag false unless a scan is running.
I9. A Visual Index run, manual or automatic, starts only when the video has scene candidates and its scan is done; with `on_index` the hook after every scan that sets the flag true enqueues a run, without a restart, only for a video that has no run at all; no automatic request replaces a run of an older pipeline version or retries a failed one; a run built with the new selection carries version 3; its scenes keep `start_time` as the frame time that the vision model is sent and the prompt and excerpt use, and carry their section in `section_start` / `section_end`.
I10. Every item of the scenes route and of `list_video_scenes` carries `frame_time`, `list_video_scenes` fetches each scene's frame at its `frame_time`, and `/status` counts native videos whose scan is pending in `pending.frame_scan` and `pending.total`, shown as the Scene change scan bar and queue row.

## Checked, no action

- **Mixing change frames into scene search and the Scenes grid.** Declined by the human.
  Scene search runs an unconstrained KNN over `vec_clip` and tag kNN does not filter by type,
  so a separate type in the same table would still change them; the change frames therefore
  get their own vector table. Mixing them in later is a copy between tables, with no
  re-embedding.
- **Putting the section into `start_time` / `end_time`.** Rejected: it would change what an
  existing column means (`.claude/rules/backend-conventions.md`), and an older build would
  then send the vision model a different frame on retry and switch its excerpt to the whole
  section. The section goes into new columns.
- **Detecting monotonous videos to lower the count.** Measured and rejected: adjacent
  representatives' CLIP similarity overlaps between a dash cam (0.65 to 0.96) and a
  timelapse whose every frame is a different era (0.51 to 0.89). The count is set by length,
  a product choice made by the human.
- **Opening and ending credits** (anime OP/ED). Telling them apart needs several episodes of
  the same series; a single video does not carry the signal. The head and tail discount is
  the only mitigation.
- **Dark scenes.** A dark scene in a film or a dark animal exhibit is content; the near-empty
  floor is relative to the file's own median and only moves the frame.
- **A long all-white or all-grey shot** (holding for two seconds or more) passes the
  steady-second rule; only momentary flashes and fades are avoided. A scan at one frame per
  second cannot see sub-second changes; a prototype reading frames 0.6 s apart behaved alike
  on the samples except for one such shot.
- **OCR area as the textual test.** Measured and rejected: a dash cam's overlay boxes cover
  7 to 18 % of the frame, and an anime frame produced a 0.998 false box. Character count
  separates screen recordings (1,281 to 2,323) and slides (305 to 1,014) from overlays
  (44 to 114).
- **Frame OCR switched off** (`features.video_frame_ocr = "false"`): no representative is
  textual, and screen recordings fall back to CLIP comparison as before this change.
- **Transcripts, corrections and OCR that arrive after a run** move boundaries but not the
  Visual Index fingerprint; such a run is not marked stale.
- **A file whose type changes to or from a native video after its flag was set** keeps its
  flag, as frame OCR's flag does today.
- **YouTube `.loft`**: scanning a remote stream, OCR and the Visual Index for `.loft` are a
  later spec.
- **Storing the selection at index time.** Not needed: the selection decodes no video, so it
  stays a request-time function and follows transcript and OCR updates.
- **A row for the scan in Index details.** It rides on Image analysis (CLIP); the human can
  ask for one later.
- **The scan running beside Whisper, CLIP and frame OCR.** Each task type has its own queue
  and worker today; the scan adds one more single worker and honours the pause. It calls the
  same loaded CLIP model as the CLIP worker, without a new lock.
- **Gating the scan by features other than `index`.** The scan is index data; the Visual
  Index, the scenes route and the file question keep their own feature gates where they read
  it.
- **Waiting for Whisper and frame OCR before a Visual Index run.** Declined by the human:
  either can stay unfinished for good (a provider error, a corrected transcript, `manual`
  frame OCR), which would block the run. A run made before they arrive lacks their topic
  boundaries and textual test until regenerated.
- **A file that stalls at the same point every time** keeps the seconds read before the stall
  and is not retried, as frame OCR does.
