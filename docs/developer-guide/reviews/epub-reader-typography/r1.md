## Touch points: places the diff reaches that the list leaves out

1. **The theme path.** `applyTheme` now calls `restyle()`, and `restyle()` calls
   `renderer.setAttribute("gap", …)` every time. foliate's
   `attributeChangedCallback` runs on every `setAttribute`, including one that
   sets the same value, and it calls `render()`. So every theme change now
   re-lays out the book. The list names "the renderer `gap` attribute" but not
   the theme toggle reaching it (see F6).
2. **The page-level shortcut sets** `epub-reader`, `epub-reader-arrows` and
   `epub-reader-fullscreen` stay live while focus is in the panel. `useFocusScope`
   counts the panel as in scope. Arrows, PageUp/PageDown and `f` all act
   (see F7). `f` is also a way into and out of full screen that starts with
   focus in the panel, which leads to F2.
3. **DismissScrim's contract.** DismissScrim treats "the popup" as the scrim's
   `nextElementSibling`. Here that sibling is a positioning wrapper that covers
   the whole book (see F1).
4. **`useFullscreen` is shared** with `VideoPlayer`, `ShellVideoPlayer` and
   `SeekBar`. The wider selector reaches them, but nothing else carries
   `data-swipe-exempt` (grep), so their behaviour does not change. No finding.
5. **Every section's `<html>` now carries `data-litloft-own-root` and the inline
   `--litloft-own-root`**, even with no setting stored. Both are inert without
   a rule, so invariant 1 holds as written. No finding.

Of these, (1), (2) and (3) are the candidates for a missing invariant.

## F1: A press on the book does not close the panel. The positioning wrapper counts as "inside the popup"

- Label: [introduced]. The code is new in `EpubPreview.tsx`.
- Invariant: 8 ("a press on the book closes the panel and turns no page").
- What I did: I read how `DismissScrim` decides what is outside the popup
  (`popup = scrimRef.current.nextElementSibling; if (popup.contains(target)) return;`).
  Its next sibling is the `<div data-swipe-exempt className="absolute inset-x-2 top-2 z-20 flex flex-col justify-end" style={{bottom: …}}>`
  wrapper. That wrapper spans the frame minus 8 px on each side, and the panel
  sits at its bottom.
  - jsdom: `fireEvent.pointerDown(panel.parentElement)` left the panel open
    (`P1 panel still open: true`).
  - Real browsers: I built a static page with the same boxes (iframe, a scrim
    button at `inset:0; z-index:10`, the wrapper at `inset 8px; z-index:20`, and
    a 200 px panel at its bottom). `elementFromPoint` over the book above the
    panel returns the **wrapper**, not the scrim, in both Chromium and WebKit.
- Observed: over the book, only the 8 px rim hits the scrim. Everywhere else
  over the book, a press lands on the wrapper, which DismissScrim treats as
  inside the popup. Nothing closes, and the book underneath gets nothing
  either.
- Why the tests are green: the "a press on the book" case fires `pointerDown`
  on `epub-typography-cover` (the scrim) directly, a point no real press
  reaches except at the rim.
- Consequence a user can hit: with the panel open, tapping the book does
  nothing. On a phone the only ways out are Aa or the 8 px edge. The cover
  that the spec says "lies over the book" is itself covered.
- Fix direction: give the wrapper `pointer-events: none` and the panel
  `pointer-events: auto`, or make the panel itself the scrim's next sibling.
  Test the press on a point over the book, for example the wrapper, or better
  `elementFromPoint` in e2e-layout, not on the scrim element. If the wrapper
  stops receiving events, check that the swipe exemption still covers the
  panel.

## F2: Leaving full screen with the panel open leaves focus on `<body>`, not on the book

- Label: [introduced]. The close-on-full-screen-change effect is new.
- Invariant: 8 ("After it closes, focus is on the book and keys turn pages"),
  and the spec: "back to the book's frame when it closes".
- What I did: in jsdom I entered pseudo full screen, opened the panel (focus
  in the panel), cleared the `readerWindow.focus` spy, then left full screen.
- Observed: the panel closed, `focus` was called 0 times, and
  `document.activeElement` was `BODY`. The effect
  `useEffect(() => setTypographyOpen(false), [fullscreen.isFullscreen])` closes
  the panel without `focusBook()`. `setFullscreen(true)` focuses the reader on
  the way *in*, but nothing does on the way *out*. ArrowRight still sent a
  turn, because `useFocusScope` counts body as in scope. Space does not
  reach the book: only the reader's own `keyAction` maps Space to a turn, and
  the parent has no Space shortcut. The Space part is inferred from the code,
  not measured.
- Reachable routes: `f` pressed with focus in the panel (the full-screen `f`
  shortcut is live), the browser's Escape under native full screen, the back
  gesture. The X button is not a route, because the scrim swallows that
  click.
- Consequence a user can hit: after leaving full screen this way, Space
  scrolls the file page instead of turning a page. Before this change,
  leaving full screen kept focus in the frame.
- Fix direction: have the close caused by the mode change hand focus back to
  the book when the panel held focus. Assert `focus` in the existing "closes
  when full screen is entered or left" test.

## F3: The `reason !== "anchor"` guard on `readingPlace` is held by no test, and a resize followed by a change loses the page

- Label: [introduced]. The code is new in `reader.js`.
- Invariant: 3.
- What I did: mutation R8 removes `&& e.detail.reason !== "anchor"` from the
  relocate listener (want=kill). The whole `typography.spec.ts` stayed green.
  I then wrote a probe that opens `styled.epub` at 0.2, turns 3 pages, keeps
  the start range, and repeats {set the viewport width to 640/1000/760/900/580/1000,
  apply a change (fontSize 5, then the defaults)} six times, checking
  `startStillShown` after each round.
- Observed: the real code gave `[true×6]` in both engines. With R8,
  Chromium gave `[true,false,false,false,false,false]` and WebKit gave
  `[false×6]`.
- Consequence a user can hit: with the guard present, none. The guard is
  load-bearing for rotating a phone, entering or leaving full screen, or
  resizing a window before changing a setting, and nothing keeps it.
- Fix direction: add a reflow before the change (`setViewportSize` or
  `setMode`) to the "keeps the page's first text on screen" matrix.

## F4: Two typography CSS rules that invariant 9 and the spec rely on are held by no test

- Label: [introduced]. `typographyCss` is new.
- Invariant: 9 ("…and what is inside them is never changed").
- What I did:
  - C4 dropped `code *` from the exclusion (want=kill). It lived in unit and
    e2e. The fixtures put the span inside `pre`, which `pre *` already covers,
    and the inline `<code id="inline">` has no child.
  - C9 dropped `!important` from `line-height` (want=kill by spec). It lived.
    The unit fixture sets line-height only on `body`.
- Consequence a user can hit: under C4, an inline highlighted snippet
  (`<code><span>`) takes the serif or sans face. Under C9, a converted book
  with class rules such as `.calibre p { line-height: 1.2 }` (specificity
  0,1,1, above the rule's 0,0,3) ignores the line-spacing setting.
- Fix direction: add `<p><code><span id="inlinespan">` and a class-scoped
  `line-height` rule to the `epubTypographyCss` fixture.

## F5: Font-size bounds are held by no test, on either side

- Label: [introduced]. The files are new.
- Invariant: 6 (invalid → defaults) and 5.
- What I did: each of these lived:
  - C1: `size < FONT_SIZE_STEPS.length` → `<=` in `core.js`.
  - L1: the same in `epubTypography.ts`.
  - N1: `disabled={step === 0}` → `step === 1`.
  - N2: `disabled={step === length - 1}` → `step === length`.
- Observed: the VALUES table tests 9, −1 and 1.5 but not 7, the first index
  past the end. No test presses − at 80 % or + at 200 %.
- Consequence a user can hit: under N2, + at 200 % sends `fontSize: 7`. The
  reader renders at the book's own size, the panel shows "NaN%", and the
  value is stored. Under L1 that stored 7 would then be read back as valid.
- Fix direction: add `{ fontSize: 7 }` to VALUES, and a panel test that the
  end steps disable their button.

## F6: Every theme change now re-lays out the book

- Label: [introduced]. Reproduced against the parent by running the same
  probe with `reader.js` from `59bb836c9`.
- Invariant: none. Bucket B.
- What I did: I turned 3 pages, toggled the theme dark/light 4 times, and
  counted `location` posts.
- Observed: `ca8f6433e` posted 12 (3 per toggle), `59bb836c9` posted 4.
  Guarding `setAttribute("gap")` behind "value changed" also gives 4. The page
  shown was unchanged in both engines.
- Consequence: a full paginator render on each theme switch, including the
  system dark-mode flip. I saw no page move.
- Fix direction: set `gap` only when it differs, or only from
  `applyTypography` and `openBook`.

## F7: Arrow keys, PageUp/PageDown and `f` act on the book while the panel is open with focus in it

- Label: [introduced]. The panel is new.
- Invariant: none declared. Bucket B, for the supervisor to judge.
- What I did: jsdom, inline mode. I opened the panel and pressed ArrowRight on
  the panel.
- Observed: `{"type":"turn","direction":"right"}` was sent while the panel
  stayed open. `f` toggles full screen from the panel, which closes it and
  leads to F2.
- Consequence: a user who reaches for the arrows while the dialog has focus
  turns pages behind it. This is harmless unless the dialog is meant to hold
  the keyboard.
- Fix direction: decide it. If the panel should own keys, disable the reader's
  shortcut sets while `typographyOpen`.
