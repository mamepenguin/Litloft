## R3-F1 — `except Exception` also wraps the fetch, so a code defect inside `_http_get_bytes` becomes a silent yt-dlp fallback, and no test can see it

`[introduced]` — reproduced at `d9ecefc`, different result (below).

`addons/media_import/backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py:409-416`
(the `try`) together with `:116-122` (`_http_get_bytes`).

The rewrite's answer to R2-F3 is to stop enumerating exception types and
instead narrow *where* they are caught. The narrowing covers one of the
two statements. `ET.fromstring` is now outside the blast radius of a
mapping bug (S2 confirms), but `_http_get_bytes` is a whole function
call inside the `try`, and everything it can raise — including anything
a future edit to it introduces — is converted into `return None` and
then into a yt-dlp call.

### Measured, both commits, same probe

A temporary test patched `_http_get_bytes` with each side effect,
patched `_yt_dlp_extract_flat` to return one entry, and called
`list_items(ref, limit=None)`:

```
### cda08bc ###
defect: AttributeError in fetch    -> returned 1 item(s), yt_dlp_calls=1
defect: TypeError in fetch         -> returned 1 item(s), yt_dlp_calls=1
real:   LookupError bad encoding   -> returned 1 item(s), yt_dlp_calls=1
real:   OSError                    -> returned 1 item(s), yt_dlp_calls=1

### d9ecefc ###
defect: AttributeError in fetch    -> raised AttributeError, yt_dlp_calls=0
defect: TypeError in fetch         -> raised TypeError,      yt_dlp_calls=0
real:   LookupError bad encoding   -> raised LookupError,    yt_dlp_calls=0
real:   OSError                    -> returned 1 item(s),    yt_dlp_calls=1
```

The `LookupError` row is a **gain**: `ET.fromstring` raises
`LookupError`, not `ParseError`, for a body whose XML declaration names
an unknown codec (`<?xml version="1.0" encoding="bogus-codec"?>`),
measured in the image. That is a real upstream-error-page shape and
`d9ecefc` did not fall back on it. The first two rows are the cost.

### Why no test constrains it

**U1**, `want=live`: replaced the body of `_http_get_bytes` with
`raise AttributeError(...)`. **Survived — 310 passed.** Every test in
the suite patches `_http_get_bytes`, so the real function is never
executed and no mutation inside it, or inside the first statement of the
`try`, is observable. (The whole suite also passes under
`docker run --network none`, which is the same fact from the other side:
nothing reaches the real fetch.)

`test_a_bug_after_the_parse_is_not_turned_into_a_fallback` is the test
that holds the scope, and it places the injected defect *after* the
`try`. Nothing places one inside it.

### Scenario

Inputs: a later edit to `_http_get_bytes` — adding a size cap, a header,
a retry — introduces a `TypeError` or an `AttributeError` on some
branch. Wrong output: every channel sync logs
`RSS listing failed for UC… (TypeError)` at WARNING and silently
succeeds through yt-dlp, at 0.4–1.6 s and several requests per
subscription instead of 0.05 s and one. The RSS path is dead for as long
as the defect lives, the suite is green, and the spec's own cost
argument against yt-dlp-only (`## Checked, no action`) is what is being
paid.

### What a test would have to assert

One test that does **not** patch `_http_get_bytes` but patches
`_youtube_opener.open` (or `urllib.request.OpenerDirector.open`) to
raise a `ValueError`, and asserts `list_items` propagates it with
`mock_flat.call_count == 0`. That both exercises the real
`_http_get_bytes` for the first time and turns the breadth of the
`except` into a decision rather than a default — the assertion R2-F3
asked for, relocated to where the rewrite left the hole.

## R3-F2 — a real feed whose entries carry no recognised `videoId` returns `[]`, and the new contract declares that to mean "the channel has no videos"

`[pre-existing]` — same reproduction, same result, at `d9ecefc`.

`addons/media_import/backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py:423-443`

The root-element guard exists because *"an error page that happens to be
well-formed XML would otherwise yield zero entries, which reads as 'no
new videos'"*. The rewrite keeps that guard and, in the same commit,
writes the opposite reading into the contract and pins it with a test:
`[]` now means the feed answered and there is nothing there.

The guard checks only `root.tag`. The entry loop skips any `<entry>`
whose `{http://www.youtube.com/xml/schemas/2015}videoId` is absent or
empty. A genuine Atom `<feed>` full of entries under a different YouTube
schema namespace therefore produces `[]` by the same mechanism the guard
was written to catch, through a door the guard does not cover.

### The probe

A temporary test served a well-formed `<feed>` with two `<entry>`
elements whose `yt:videoId` sat under
`http://www.youtube.com/xml/schemas/2026`, with
`_yt_dlp_extract_flat` patched to return one entry:

```
cda08bc:  PROBE result=[] yt_dlp_calls=0
d9ecefc:  PROBE result=[] yt_dlp_calls=0
```

Identical at both commits, so the mechanism is `[pre-existing]` and by
R-4 scope it belongs in the ledger, not in this change. What
`cda08bc` adds is that `[]` is now a **declared, tested answer** rather
than an unexamined return value, so the next reader has a docstring
telling them the case is handled.

### Scenario

Inputs: YouTube bumps the feed schema namespace, or drops `yt:videoId`
in favour of the Atom `<id>yt:video:…</id>` that sits beside it.
Wrong output: `list_items` returns `[]` for every channel. yt-dlp is
never tried. `_sync_diff` records `added=0, total_new=0`, advances
`last_synced_at`, clears `cooldown_until`
(`manager.py:367-372`). Every subscription reports healthy and imports
nothing, indefinitely and silently — the exact outcome invariant 4 and
the root-tag guard exist to prevent, reached from outside both.

This is the same class of upstream change the whole PR is a reaction to,
which is why it is worth the ledger line rather than being left in this
file.

### What a test would have to assert

A feed with `n` `<entry>` elements none of which yields an item is not
the same observation as a feed with zero `<entry>` elements. Asserting
that `_list_channel_via_rss` returns `None` (not `[]`) when
`root.findall(entry)` is non-empty and `headers` is empty would
distinguish them; no test does today.

## R3-F3 — the fallback warning no longer says a fallback happened, so a recovered sync and a failed one open with the same line

`[introduced]` — the format string is changed by `cda08bc`.

`addons/media_import/backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py:413-415`

`d9ecefc` logged
`RSS listing failed for %s (%s); falling back to yt-dlp`. `cda08bc`
logs `RSS listing failed for %s (%s)`. Measured end to end under
`caplog` at both commits:

```
### cda08bc ###
RSS down, yt-dlp OK    -> returned 1 item
   WARNING  RSS listing failed for UCabc… (HTTPError)
RSS down, yt-dlp empty -> raised _ListingUnavailable: UCabc…
   WARNING  RSS listing failed for UCabc… (HTTPError)

### d9ecefc ###
RSS down, yt-dlp OK    -> returned 1 item
   WARNING  RSS listing failed for UCabc… (HTTPError); falling back to yt-dlp
RSS down, yt-dlp empty -> raised HTTPError: HTTP Error 404: Not Found
   WARNING  RSS listing failed for UCabc… (HTTPError); falling back to yt-dlp
```

The **exception** side of this is an improvement and is worth saying so:
r2 recorded that `raise exc` made the operator read an RSS 404 for an
outcome yt-dlp actually decided. `_ListingUnavailable` names the real
outcome, and nothing upstream branches on the type — `worker._execute`
catches bare `Exception` (`worker.py:180`) and no code anywhere
classifies by exception class (grepped: no `except urllib.error…`, no
`HTTPError` reference outside `youtube.py`). So the change of type is
inert upstream and clearer in the log.

The **warning** side is the regression. During the outage this change
targets, all 51 channel subscriptions emit this WARNING every hour
whether or not the sync recovered. `docs/addons/media-import.md`'s
troubleshooting row sends the operator to
`docker compose logs backend` to find the cause; the line they arrive at
now reports a failure for a sync that worked. Distinguishing the two
requires noticing the *absence* of a later `_ListingUnavailable`
traceback.

No declared invariant covers logging, so this is a B.

### What a test would have to assert

Nothing — this is not worth a test. The remedy is three words back in
the format string, or demoting the recovered case to INFO.

## The questions a mutation does not answer

### Everything reachable inside the `try`, and whether anything non-transport lands there

The `try` covers exactly two statements. Walked for this scope, not
cited from r2 (whose `try` enclosed the whole method):

**`_http_get_bytes(url)`** → `_youtube_opener.open(url, timeout=10.0)`
+ `resp.read()`. `url` is built in this function from `channel_id`,
which `list_items` has already matched against
`^UC[A-Za-z0-9_-]{22}$` before calling, so `Request.full_url`'s
`ValueError("unknown url type")` is unreachable and the
`FileHandler`/`FTPHandler` in the default opener set cannot be entered.
`_YouTubeOnlyRedirectHandler.redirect_request` raises `HTTPError` (an
`OSError`) for an off-host redirect. The reachable set is therefore
`urllib.error.HTTPError` / `URLError` (both `OSError`),
`http.client.*` (`HTTPException`), `TimeoutError` and `ssl.SSLError`
(both `OSError`), and `MemoryError` for a body large enough to exhaust
the process — `resp.read()` has no size cap. All transport except
`MemoryError`, which `d9ecefc` also did not catch and which yt-dlp
would then meet as well.

**`ET.fromstring(body)`** — parses bytes already in memory, does no
I/O. Measured in the image across eight bodies: `ParseError` for empty,
truncated, binary, undefined-entity and external-entity input (ET does
not resolve external entities, so the `file:///etc/passwd` case is a
`ParseError`, not a read); `LookupError` for an unknown encoding
declaration; UTF-16 and a one-level internal entity parse cleanly. The
only non-`ParseError` shape is `LookupError`, and falling back on it is
right.

So **nothing non-transport arises inside the `try` today**, and the
breadth of `except Exception` is currently inert in production. The
exposure is not what the code can raise now but what an edit to
`_http_get_bytes` will raise later, with no test able to see it —
R3-F1.

### Does anything upstream treat `_ListingUnavailable` differently?

**No.** `list_items` has one production call site,
`SubscriptionManager._sync_diff` (`manager.py:317`), which does not
catch it; `_sync_blocking` propagates; `worker._execute`
(`worker.py:180`) catches bare `Exception`, calls `logger.exception`,
applies `_apply_cron_backoff` when `job.kind == "cron"`, and broadcasts
`error="exception"`. No code in the addon classifies by exception type
(`ERROR_RATE_LIMITED` / `ERROR_PERMANENT` are set from yt-dlp message
text in `fetch_item`, not from listing failures). Changing the escaping
type from `HTTPError` to `_ListingUnavailable` is inert upstream. The
log is dealt with in R3-F3.

### Is there an input for which a channel that genuinely has no videos can never be synced?

**Yes, and it is unchanged from `d9ecefc` and deliberate.** Empty
channel with a healthy feed → valid `<feed>`, zero `<entry>`, `[]`
returned, yt-dlp untouched (`test_empty_feed_is_answered_without_the_fallback`,
and mutation P1 confirms the `[]` cannot be collapsed into `None`).
Empty channel while the feed is down → RSS returns `None`, yt-dlp
correctly returns nothing, `_ListingUnavailable` is raised on every
attempt for the duration of the outage. Revised invariant 4 chooses that
over trusting an empty answer from a source that cannot distinguish "no
videos" from "bot check". Where the row has never synced, R2-F2
applies — not re-reported.

R3-F2 adds a third input to this list that is *not* deliberate: a feed
that answers with entries the mapper cannot read reports "no videos"
forever, and unlike the two above it reports success while doing so.

### Detector rules on the four new / changed tests

`review-workflow.md` "Detector rules" checked against
`test_yt_dlp_fallback_when_limit_exceeds_rss` (changed),
`test_empty_fallback_raises_rather_than_reporting_no_videos` (renamed +
one assertion), `test_a_bug_after_the_parse_is_not_turned_into_a_fallback`
and `test_empty_feed_is_answered_without_the_fallback` (new).
**No violations.** Every assertion is an exact `==` against a value
written in the test (`call_count == 0`, `== 1`, `== []`,
`== 50`, the two declared id lists); none is derived from the run; none
reads source as text; no `>=`.

One observation that is *not* a rule violation and not a repeat of
R2-F4: in `test_empty_feed_is_answered_without_the_fallback` the
`mock_flat.call_count == 0` line is not what kills P1 or P4 — the
declared `== []` is, because the collapsed path raises
`_ListingUnavailable` before reaching it. The line is redundant rather
than load-bearing-by-accident, and the assertion that does the work is
a declared value, so it does not decay the way R2-F4's did.

### Tree

`git -C addons/media_import status --short` is clean. The two temporary
probe files were deleted; nothing was fixed.

## Trajectory — did this round remove a distinction, or rename one?

> *Count what the rewrite deleted and what it introduced, and say which
> way the balance falls.*

**It removed one. The balance is not close, and the count is not the
reason — the reason is that the construct which grew in rounds one and
two no longer exists to grow.**

### The three diffs, by shape of the implementation change

```
460298d  +21 −2    backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py
d9ecefc  +14 −2    backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py
cda08bc  +32 −31   backend/subscription/providers/youtube.py
```

`cda08bc` is the first round whose deletions are of the same order as
its additions. That is a symptom, not the argument.

### What disappeared

- **`class _FeedUnavailable`** — an exception whose only job was to
  carry one failure shape from the middle of `_list_channel_via_rss` to
  a handler eleven lines away.
- **`raise _FeedUnavailable(root.tag)`** and the `except` arm that
  caught it — a raise/catch pair *inside one module, across two
  methods*, collapsed into `return None`.
- **The four-member exception tuple**, and with it the question *"which
  exception types mean the feed failed?"*. This is the construct that
  grew: three members at `460298d`, five at `d9ecefc` (counting
  `OSError` subsuming `URLError`), and each growth was a reviewer naming
  a shape the author had not predicted.
- **`import http.client`** from the implementation — the import existed
  only to name a member of that tuple.
- **`raise exc`** — a captured exception carried as state across the
  fallback call, and (per r2) misattributing the outcome when it fired.

### What appeared

- **`class _ListingUnavailable`** — one exception class, replacing one
  exception class. Net zero, and its role narrowed: `_FeedUnavailable`
  named an *intermediate* RSS shape; `_ListingUnavailable` names the
  *final* outcome and is raised at the one place the outcome is known.
- **`list[ItemHeader] | None`** — one new distinction in the return
  type, and the only one added.
- **`if headers is not None:`** — one branch at the call site,
  replacing the `except` arm that stood there. The call site still has
  exactly two branches (`headers is not None`, `if not headers`), as it
  did at `d9ecefc`.
- **A second `logger.warning`** — the non-feed case now logs where it
  is detected instead of at the handler. One call added, one message
  lost (R3-F3).

### Is `_ListingUnavailable` + `None`/`[]` fewer moving parts than the tuple + `_FeedUnavailable` + the re-raise?

Yes, and the test that settles it is not the count but the **growth
law**. Adding a sixth way for the feed to fail used to require editing
the tuple — that is what rounds one and two each did. Under the
rewrite it requires editing nothing: a new shape lands in
`except Exception`, becomes `None`, and takes the path that was already
there. The enumeration did not shrink; it stopped being an enumeration.

The honest objection is that "stopped being an enumeration" and "became
total" are the same sentence. `except Exception` is the terminal point
of the growth, not an escape from it, and r2 ran exactly that as a
surviving mutant (N12). What makes it convergence rather than
capitulation is the second half of the same commit: the `try` shrank
from *the whole method* to *two statements*, and that boundary is
tested in both directions (S2 and S3 both kill). The design now bounds
what may be swallowed by **where** rather than by **what**, and *where*
is a two-line region that does not grow when upstream invents a new
failure. R3-F1 is the price of the half of that boundary that is one
statement too wide.

Two further observations bearing on the call, neither of which I
decide:

- **The rewrite did not touch round one's other change.** R2-F2 coupled
  `d9ecefc`'s re-raise to `460298d`'s cap and ordering. `cda08bc`
  raises in the same circumstances, so the coupling is intact and
  unaddressed; it is in `known-issues.md` by decision.
- **Two of round two's four findings are closed by construction, not by
  a new branch.** R2-F1 and R2-F4 were both "the assertion does not
  hold what its name says", and both were fixed by making an existing
  assertion load-bearing (`call_count == 1`, `return_value=_RSS_SAMPLE`)
  rather than by adding a test for a newly-named case. That is the
  opposite of the pattern r2 described.

My three findings are all B: one is the cost of the new boundary
(R3-F1), one predates the change and is inherited by its contract
(R3-F2), one is three words in a log line (R3-F3). No finding adds a
case the previous round did not anticipate, because none of them is
about a failure shape.

TOTAL: 3 findings
