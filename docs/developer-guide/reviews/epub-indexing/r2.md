### What the byte budget does to real books (measured)

The XHTML byte total (decompressed `.xhtml`/`.html` members) of the 54 EPUBs on this machine: largest 5.64 MiB (`SwiftUI Views Mastery`, 530 files), Japanese novels 0.4-0.9 MiB, none over 20 MiB. Three of them run through the extractor at `c36b5ed` and again with `BOOK_MAX_BYTES = 10**12` produce identical chunks and titles (SwiftUI Views Mastery, Advanced SwiftUI Views Mastery, 改訂新版 Visual Studio Code実践ガイド).

`SwiftUI Views Mastery` yields 447,874 characters from 5.64 MiB, 13.2 bytes per character. At that ratio the byte budget stops a book at about 1.59 M characters, before `TEXT_MAX_CHARS` (2 M). So for markup-heavy technical books the byte budget, not the character budget, is the binding text cap; a book of that style would need to be about 3.5 times the size of the largest one here to reach it.

A spent budget does not change numbering (`book.sections` is built before the loop). TOC titles are still assigned to every section after the cut, and those sections have no chunks: the same state the character budget already produced, and consistent with invariants 4 and 5 (the row names the right section; nothing without a chunk shows a pill or citation). In a book without a TOC, heading titles stop at the cut, along with the chunks.

## Trajectory

Round 1 is the original implementation; round 2 is one fix round, so no two fix rounds exist to compare. Read in order:

- `29654c6` **adds a state and a branch**: a second budget (`bytes_left`) and a `break`, in the same loop that already carried the character budget (`budget`) and the per-member cap. It is a new cap, not a special case for one input, but the loop now has two budgets that gate different reads (`budget` gates text reads, `bytes_left` gates every read), and their interaction is where the one survivor of this round sits (F1). F2 is a read the new budget does not see.
- `6d6bf89` **removes** a prediction: a title lookup in the defensive branch becomes a constant.
- `b173bc7` merges two copies of the label choice into one variable; no new branch.

So: one of three fixes adds state, two remove or merge. Not yet the patching pattern. If a round-3 fix in `_extract_sections` adds another charge site or exception for a kind of read (heading-only reads, reads that raise), that would be the second round in a row adding state to that loop; charging at the one place bytes are decompressed (`_read_member`) would cover both without a new branch.

## Findings

### F1. The byte budget's charge on heading-only reads is not held; the surviving mutant brings back r1-F1 for books without a TOC

- Label: `[introduced]` (`29654c6`, new check and new test). Invariant: 1, as r1-F1 (the book stalls the text worker). The code at `c36b5ed` is correct; this is a test gap.
- Evidence: every row of `test_book_budget_stops_further_reads` has a nav TOC whose first entry is section 1, so `wants_heading` is false for every section and every read is a text read. Mutant B6 (charge nothing when `wants_text` is false) passes the whole EPUB suite.
- Reproduction (test image): a book with no TOC, section 1 = 2.1 M characters of text (spends `TEXT_MAX_CHARS`), then 30 spine items naming one 4.9 MB member of `<div><span></span></div>`; 15,378 bytes on disk. At `c36b5ed`: 4 reads of the member, 8.4 s. Under B6: 30 reads, 62.1 s (about 1.8 s per heading parse), so about 1 hour at `SECTION_MAX` = 2,000.
- A row with no TOC and a spent character budget, asserting the read count, would hold it.

### F2. A read that raises inside `_read_member` is not charged to the byte budget

- Label: `[introduced]` (`29654c6`). Invariant: none (1 and 2 hold; the cost is small).
- Evidence: `bytes_left -= ...` runs after `_read_member` returns. When the member fails its CRC or its deflate stream is bad, `zipfile` raises after decompressing, the `except` skips the section, and nothing is charged.
- Reproduction (test image): 2,000 spine items naming one 4.9 MB member whose central-directory CRC is flipped; 24,585 bytes on disk. All 2,000 reads happen (about 9.3 GB decompressed), 0 chunks, 4.5 s in total. No parse runs on this path, which is what made r1-F1 expensive, so the bound that matters still holds.
- Recorded because the commit's claim ("stop reading once 20 MB have been read") does not hold for this path; see the trajectory note on where the charge sits.

### F3. The nav and heading half of the ruby-title test is held by bs4, not by the code

- Label: `[introduced]` (`c36b5ed` test). Invariant: none (3 covers chunks; titles are not in it).
- Evidence: bs4 4.15 (the version the test image installs) leaves `<rt>`/`<rp>` text out of `get_text()` for HTML-parsed documents, but not for `"xml"`. Probe in the image: `BeautifulSoup(..., "lxml").find("a").get_text()` on `<ruby>漢字<rp>(</rp><rt>かんじ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>の章` returns `漢字の章` with the `<rt>` still in the tree. So under mutant T4b (decompose only for `"xml"`), `test_title_drops_ruby_readings[nav]` and the `前書き漢字` heading assertion in both rows still pass; only the NCX title fails. `requirements.txt` allows `beautifulsoup4>=4.12`, whose `get_text()` includes ruby text.
- Same shape as r1-security F1: the protection is the dependency's, and the test was fixed there by substituting the parser. Consequence if the decompose were narrowed and bs4 were older: nav and heading titles read `漢字(かんじ)の章`. Deleting the loop outright is caught (T4).
