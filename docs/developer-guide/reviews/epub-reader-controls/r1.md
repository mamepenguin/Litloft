## F1: The seek-landing test compares against the fraction the seek saved, so it cannot see where the seek landed

- Label: [introduced] (test and `seekTarget` are new in 390e4fa81)
- Invariant: 3 (a seek lands on the same page a fresh open at the saved fraction shows)
- What I did: two mutations in `frontend/public/epub-reader/reader.js`, each the regression the spec names ("does not call `view.goToFraction`: that skips the `restoreAnchor` correction and lands a page off"):
  - M11: `seekTarget` returns `{ index, anchor: inSection }` (no `restoreAnchor`).
  - M12: `runPendingSeek` does `turn((v) => v.goToFraction(fraction))`.
  Both LIVE against `reader.spec.ts` (all seek tests pass in Chromium and WebKit).
- Why: `seek in <book> › reports one turn, and lands where reopening at that place lands` reopens at `turned.fraction` — the start-of-page fraction of wherever the seek happened to land — and restore is self-consistent for that value, so any landing passes. It never compares with a fresh open at the fraction that was *sent*.
- Repro: a scratch spec (since deleted) that runs `seek(f)` then `open(book, f)` for f in {0.13, 0.37, 0.55, 0.71, 0.93} on horizontal and vertical: 20/20 pass at HEAD; with M11 or M12 applied, 12/20 fail (Chromium and WebKit, both books).
- What a user hits if this regresses unseen: after releasing the slider the book lands a page off from where the same fraction reopens, and the thumb jumps on release (the reported `location.fraction` differs from the dragged value).
- Direction: in the existing test, reopen at the seek's input fraction (0.55), not at `turned.fraction`, or assert both. Also think about a second fraction; 0.55 happened to pass in some combos of the scratch run.

## F3: While dragging, the label names a TOC entry that points at a non-linear section instead of the chapter the thumb is in

- Label: [introduced] (`chapterAt`, `flattenToc` are new)
- Invariant: none (label only; nothing saved or navigated wrongly)
- What I did: in the scratch book above, the `ready.toc` observed was `[One 0, Two 0.333, Notes 1]` — the non-linear `notes` section gets `sectionFractions[4]`, which is 1 (SectionProgress gives non-linear sections size 0, so its start equals the next linear start, or 1 at the end). `chapterAt(toc, 1)` therefore returns "Notes" while `seek(1)` lands in c3, whose reported chapter (`location.tocIndex`) is "Two". The same arithmetic gives a non-linear cover that heads the TOC fraction 0, equal to chapter 1's, and `chapterAt` keeps the first of equal fractions, so dragging anywhere in chapter 1 would read "Cover" (derived from `SectionProgress` sizes, not separately run).
- Consequence: the dragged label disagrees with the label shown after release in books that list a cover or notes in their TOC. Cosmetic.
- Direction: give an entry whose section is non-linear (size 0) `fraction: null` in `flattenToc`, or have `chapterAt` skip entries whose section size is 0. (PR-3's TOC panel will want the same rule for "selectable".)

## F4: Leaving full screen in the middle of a drag leaves the chrome held for good; on the next full screen the bar never hides

- Label: [introduced] (`scrubbing` / `held` wiring and `EpubPositionBar` are new)
- Invariant: none of the seven (see "missing from the list" below)
- What I did: a scratch vitest (kept outside the tree at `scratchpad/review/mut/zz-scratch.test.tsx.txt`, removed from the worktree) on `EpubPreview`: open, `location`, enter full screen, `fireEvent.change` on the slider (drag under way, `onScrubbingChange(true)`), leave full screen (the full-screen `EpubPositionBar` unmounts with `drag !== null`, so `onScrubbingChange(false)` never runs), enter full screen again, advance 5 s.
- Observed: `epub-chrome-bottom` is still not `inert` (test S1 fails at HEAD). Control S1b — same sequence without the change event — hides as expected.
- How a user reaches it: in full screen, hold the mouse on the slider and press `Esc` (the slider is an INPUT, but the full-screen `escape` shortcut is `editingOnly: false`, so it fires), or anything else that exits full screen mid-drag (native fullscreen exit by the browser). `scrubbing` stays `true` until another drag ends, so the next full-screen session never withdraws its chrome and the bar covers the bottom of the book.
- Whether a real browser fires `blur` on the unmounting input (which would call `commit(false)` and clear it) is not settled by jsdom; measure it — but `commit` runs `setDrag` on an unmounting component, so even a blur is not a guarantee.
- Direction: clear the hold when the bar unmounts (e.g. an effect cleanup in `EpubPositionBar` that calls `onScrubbingChange(false)`), or reset `scrubbing` in `EpubPreview` whenever `fullscreen.isFullscreen` changes.

## F6: The parent's per-entry TOC drop re-numbers the list, so `tocIndex` (foliate's id) can then name the wrong chapter

- Label: [introduced] (`parseToc`, `chapterOf` are new)
- Invariant: none today (see reachability)
- What I did: read `parseToc` against `flattenToc` and `chapterOf`. `tocIndex` is foliate's pre-order id, which equals a position in the *reader's* list (M17 kills that). `parseToc` then `continue`s past any entry it rejects, so every later entry moves up one: with reader list `[A, bad, C]` the parent holds `[A, C]`, a `location` with `tocIndex: 1` (the bad entry's id) is shown as "C", and `tocIndex: 2` falls through to `chapterAt`. M21 (drop the whole list on a bad entry instead) is killed only by the tests that assert "drops … and keeps the rest", i.e. the tests pin the re-numbering.
- Reachability: today the reader only emits entries the parser accepts (label sliced to the same `TOC_LABEL_MAX`, integer depth, fraction from `sectionFractions` in [0,1] or null), so no drop happens. It becomes reachable the moment the two caps or checks drift, and PR-3 will navigate by this same index (`goToToc { index }`), where a shift sends the reader to the wrong chapter rather than mislabeling one.
- Direction: keep positions stable — replace a rejected entry with a placeholder (`{label: "", depth: 0, fraction: null}`) instead of skipping it, or drop the whole `toc` (not `ready`) when any entry is bad.

- Direction: an `e2e-layout` fixture for the bar at those sizes.

## R-0 invariants: does any path break them, and what is missing

- 1 (no write on restore/resize/location): held. M45 (save on `location`) and M46 (reader posts `turned` beside `location`) are killed; the relayout e2e checks no `turned`.
- 2 (a seek after the start writes one `turned`): held; M13 (seek outside `turn()`) killed.
- 3 (seek lands where a fresh open lands): holds in the code for the fractions I tried (scratch: 20/20), but is not held by the tests — F1, F2.
  - As worded it is false by design for a seek to 1 (or any seek that lands on the last page): the seek shows the last page, `turned.atEnd` saves 1, and a fresh open at 1 goes to the start ("a book you left on its last page reopens at the start"). Suggest scoping it to fractions in (0, 1) that do not land on the last page.
- 4 (no href/markup reaches the parent; labels are text): held. `ready` carries only label/depth/fraction (e2e checks no `<`, `xhtml`, `href`); labels render as React text (`<b>One</b>` test). The hrefs stay in `state.tocHrefs`.
- 5 (bad TOC still opens): held; `toc.epub` e2e plus M14/M18/M20 killed. `buildToc`'s own try/catch is unreachable in practice (flattenToc already guards the resolver), so deleting it would survive; not mutated.
- 6 (sanitizer/CSP unchanged): held; neither `sanitize.js` nor `epubReaderCsp.ts` is in the diff.
- 7 (inline bar height stable): holds by construction as far as reading can tell; no test — F7.

Missing from the list (round-one question):
- **In full screen, showing or hiding the chrome never re-lays out the book** (no `location` with a changed page on a middle tap / mouse move). The spec argues for overlay precisely for this; invariant 7 covers only inline.
- **The chrome is not withdrawn during a drag, and nothing leaves it held after the drag ends** (F4 breaks the second half).
- **After a pointer release on the slider, the page-turn keys turn pages again** (focus back to the book). It is in the spec's keyboard section and has a test, but it is a user-facing promise, not just an implementation detail.
